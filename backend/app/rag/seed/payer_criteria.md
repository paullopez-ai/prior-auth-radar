# Synthetic Payer Prior Authorization Criteria

Synthetic, demonstration-only payer criteria for common high-volume procedures.
These are illustrative patterns for the RAG layer, not real payer policy. They
capture documentation requirements, common denial reasons, and peer-to-peer
success patterns by procedure family and payer type.

## MRI and Advanced Imaging (e.g., CPT 70553 Brain MRI)

Documentation requirements: signed ordering-provider note establishing the
clinical indication; relevant prior imaging or labs; for neurologic MRI,
documentation of focal symptoms, progressive headache with red-flag features, or
failed initial workup. Most commercial payers approve diagnostic MRI with a clear
indication and have high approval rates for brain MRI in the setting of
progressive headache with visual disturbance.

Common denial reasons: missing clinical indication, request for repeat imaging
within a short interval without new findings, or imaging ordered before a
required initial evaluation. Approved imaging authorizations are typically time-
limited; if the authorization expiration date is near the scheduled study date,
confirm the appointment well before expiration because a lapsed authorization
requires full resubmission.

## Orthopedic Surgery — Joint Replacement (CPT 27447 Knee, 27130 Hip, 23472 Shoulder)

Documentation requirements: imaging confirming advanced degenerative joint
disease (e.g., Kellgren-Lawrence grade 3-4 osteoarthritis); documented failure of
conservative management — typically a minimum of 6 to 12 weeks of supervised
physical therapy, anti-inflammatory medication, and where appropriate
intra-articular corticosteroid injections; functional assessment documenting
impact on activities of daily living.

Common denial reasons: insufficient documentation of conservative-treatment
failure is the single most common cause of denial for elective joint replacement.
Payers frequently deny when PT duration is below the required threshold or when
injection history is not documented with dates and outcomes.

Peer-to-peer success pattern: when a denial is for medical necessity and the
patient in fact has extensive conservative-treatment history that was missing
from the original submission, a peer-to-peer review with the complete record has
a high reversal rate for orthopedic procedures. Lead the peer-to-peer with a
concise chronological summary: PT duration and outcome, injection dates and
relief, current functional limitations, and the surgeon's assessment that
conservative options are exhausted.

Hip and shoulder revision and arthroplasty cases for elderly patients with
prosthesis loosening or severe glenohumeral or coxofemoral osteoarthritis carry
strong medical necessity; the limiting factor is usually documentation
completeness and, for urgent cases, payer response time.

## Spine Surgery (CPT 63047 Laminectomy, 22612 Lumbar Fusion)

Documentation requirements: recent lumbar MRI (generally within 90 days)
demonstrating the structural correlate (stenosis, herniation, instability);
documented neurologic findings; documentation of at least 6 weeks of conservative
treatment including physical therapy and medication management; for fusion,
evidence of instability or deformity.

Common denial reasons: lumbar fusion has stringent criteria and a higher denial
rate than decompression alone. Denials commonly cite absent instability
documentation or insufficient conservative care. Progressive neurologic deficits
(e.g., lower-extremity weakness) support medical urgency and strengthen the case.

Best approach: submit the operative-planning MRI with the radiologist
interpretation, comprehensive PT records with dates and outcomes, and a letter of
medical necessity citing the specific ICD-10 diagnosis and the structural finding.
When a payer is also in violation of a CMS response window, lead with the
compliance violation while simultaneously assembling the clinical package.

## Gastrointestinal Procedures (CPT 43239 Upper GI Endoscopy, 43280 Fundoplication)

Diagnostic upper GI endoscopy with biopsy for dysphagia or suspected esophageal
pathology has consistently high approval rates when the clinical indication is
documented. These are rarely denied.

Laparoscopic fundoplication for severe GERD with documented esophageal damage
(e.g., Barrett's esophagus) is generally medically necessary, but is a frequent
site-of-service denial: some payers require an inpatient or hospital-based setting
rather than an ambulatory surgical center for specific procedure codes.

Site-of-service denials are procedural, not clinical. They resolve when the
provider either (1) presents clinical justification for the requested setting in a
peer-to-peer, or (2) accommodates the payer's preferred facility. The underlying
procedure approval is usually not in question, which makes the expected outcome
medium rather than low.

## Critical Care and Emergency Inpatient (CPT 99291)

Critical care services during an active emergency admission (e.g., acute
respiratory failure) have very high approval rates and qualify for the urgent
72-hour window. Ensure attending-physician documentation clearly establishes the
medical necessity of critical-care-level services. If the payer does not respond
within the 72-hour urgent window, escalate via the CMS urgent-PA compliance
process and document the violation.

## Payer-Type Patterns

- Medicare Advantage plans are subject to the strictest CMS response-time
  enforcement. Urgent MA cases that exceed 72 hours are clear, citable
  violations.
- When multiple violations involve the same payer family, suspect a systemic
  processing delay rather than isolated incidents, and consider a consolidated
  escalation.
- A standardized pre-submission documentation checklist for surgical PAs prevents
  the majority of medical-necessity denials at the source.
