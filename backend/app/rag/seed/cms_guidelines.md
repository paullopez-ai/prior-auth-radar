# CMS Prior Authorization Final Rule (CMS-0057-F) — Operational Guidelines

Effective January 1, 2026. Applies to Medicare Advantage organizations, state
Medicaid and CHIP fee-for-service programs, Medicaid and CHIP managed care
plans, and Qualified Health Plan issuers on the Federally Facilitated Exchanges.
This document summarizes the response-time, transparency, and documentation
requirements most relevant to medical practice prior authorization operations.

## Response Time Windows

Standard (non-urgent) prior authorization requests must receive a decision within
seven (7) calendar days of the payer receiving the request. The clock starts on
the date the payer receives a complete request and runs on calendar days, not
business days. Weekends and holidays count toward the seven-day window.

Expedited (urgent) prior authorization requests must receive a decision within
seventy-two (72) hours — that is, three (3) calendar days. A request qualifies as
urgent when applying the standard time frame could seriously jeopardize the life
or health of the patient or the patient's ability to regain maximum function.
Critical care, emergency inpatient admissions, and time-sensitive surgical cases
generally meet the urgent standard.

If the response deadline passes with no decision communicated to the requesting
provider, the payer is in violation of the CMS Prior Authorization Final Rule.
This is a documented non-compliance event, not merely a delayed response. The
practice should escalate immediately rather than continuing to wait.

## Escalation Procedure for Missed Deadlines

When a payer exceeds the applicable response window, do not simply "contact the
payer." File a CMS non-compliance escalation:

1. Call the payer's provider relations line and reference the specific
   authorization number and submission date.
2. Cite the CMS Prior Authorization Final Rule (CMS-0057-F) and state the exact
   number of days the payer is overdue relative to the 7-day standard or 72-hour
   urgent window.
3. Document the date, time, representative name, and reference number from the
   call.
4. If unresolved, prepare a CMS non-compliance complaint using the call
   documentation. Medicare Advantage non-compliance can be reported to the CMS
   regional office and through the plan's grievance process.

A documented compliance violation creates regulatory leverage. Payers facing a
cited violation frequently expedite the decision to avoid a formal complaint.

## Denial Requirements

Payers must provide a specific reason for every denial. A denial that merely
states "not medically necessary" without identifying the unmet clinical criterion
is itself deficient under the rule. The denial notice must include the specific
clinical or coverage criteria that were not met and inform the provider of appeal
rights.

When a denial appears to rest on insufficient chart review rather than a
categorical coverage exclusion, a physician-to-physician peer-to-peer review is
the strongest reversal path. Peer-to-peer reviews reverse a substantial share of
medical-necessity denials for orthopedic and surgical procedures when complete
conservative-treatment documentation is presented.

## Appeal Rights and Timelines

Denied prior authorizations carry appeal rights. Standard appeals generally must
be filed within the timeframe stated on the denial notice; many commercial payers
set a 30 to 60 day window, while the actionable peer-to-peer window is often much
shorter (frequently 48 hours to 14 days from the denial). Track the appeal
deadline on every denied PA. Missing the appeal deadline forfeits the fastest
reversal path and may require resubmission as a new request.

## Transparency and Reporting

The rule requires impacted payers to publicly report aggregate prior
authorization metrics, including approval and denial rates, the percentage of
requests approved after appeal, and average decision times. Practices can use
published payer metrics to anticipate likely outcomes and to support escalation
when a payer's behavior deviates from its own reported performance.

## Practice Operational Takeaways

- Submit complete documentation up front. Most surgical denials trace to missing
  conservative-treatment records, not categorical exclusions.
- Compute urgency relative to the scheduled procedure date. A PA that is pending
  within five days of an unresolved procedure is critical regardless of other
  factors.
- Treat every missed response window as a compliance event and document it.
- Distinguish medical-necessity denials (peer-to-peer candidates) from site-of-
  service denials (often resolved by accommodating the payer's facility
  preference).
